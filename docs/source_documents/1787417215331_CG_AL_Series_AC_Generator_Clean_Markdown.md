# 1.0 General Information

The AL-Series alternators are manufactured using accurate methods of design, construction and test. Their characteristics, if properly used, ensure continuous working and a very prolonged trouble-free life. For the best use of AL-Series alternators, the manual recommends following the installation and operating instructions carefully.

## 1.1 Specifications

AL-Series alternators are self-excited, self-regulated and supplied with either an electronic voltage regulator or compound excitation transformer.

They comply with the following standards:

- ISO 9001
- BS 5000
- IS 4722 and 13364
- CSA — Canadian Standard Association (1995)

## 1.2 Mechanical Features

- Aluminium body with integral N.D.E. shield, up to 200 frame.
- Cast iron D.E. shield.
- Life-lubricated sealed ball bearings, up to 6313 size.
- Screen-protected, self-ventilated machine.
- Degree of protection: **IP21**; IP23 optional.

## 1.3 Normal Operating Conditions

- Altitude: less than **1000 m (3300 ft)**.
- Ambient temperature: less than **40 °C**.
- Power factor: from **0.8 lagging up to UPF**.

## 1.4 Limits for Safe Operation

- Overspeed: **20% (1800 rpm for 50 Hz)**.
- Overload: **110% of rated value for one hour**.

## 1.5 Electrical Features

- Class H insulation as standard.
- Motor starting capability: alternator capable of starting a **1.0 HP induction motor** for each kVA rating with DOL. Motor starting is applicable only for S1 duty.
- Steady-state voltage regulation:
  - ±5% with compound excitation unit.
  - ±1% with electronic regulator.
- At rated speed, when supplying non-distorting, three-phase balanced linear loads, regulation is within the stated limits.
- Voltage build-up is based on residual magnetism.

## 1.6 Output Voltage and Frequency

Standard alternators are designed for:

- **415 V at 50 Hz** in three phase.
- **240 V at 50 Hz** in single phase.

# 2.0 Principle of Operation

When started, residual magnetism induces a voltage in the exciter armature. This voltage is rectified by rotating diodes and fed to the main field.

The induced voltage in the auxiliary winding is then used to increase excitation power through the compound excitation (CE) unit / electronic regulator (AVR) to the exciter field, ensuring rapid and smooth build-up of output voltage in the main stator winding.

On load, overload or short circuit, the `L` winding of the CE unit, which is in series with the load, supplies an additional excitation voltage (booster effect) to maintain terminal voltage within approximately ±5% of rated value.

For alternators fitted with electronic regulators (AVR SR 7/3 and UVR-6), the AVR provides additional excitation and maintains terminal voltage within approximately ±1% from no-load to rated-load condition.

# 4.0 Safety Requirements

Before any cleaning, lubrication or maintenance operation:

- Ensure that the gen-set is stationary.
- Disconnect it from the power supply.
- Follow the prescribed procedure for stopping the prime mover.

The alternator itself has no emergency stop; stopping is controlled by the device arranged by the installer to stop the prime mover.

Before installing the alternator:

- Provide suitable earthing in compliance with relevant electrical regulations.
- Ensure that the grounding system is in good condition.
- Follow the regulations of the country where the generator is installed.

### Installer responsibility

> **CAUTION:** The final installer is responsible for installation of all protections, including sectioning devices, protection against direct and indirect contacts, overcurrent and overvoltage protections, emergency stop, and other protections necessary for compliance with applicable international safety regulations.

### Handling and lifting

- For unpacked alternators, use the specified lifting eyebolts and suitable ropes.
- Do not lift the alternator too far from the floor; the manual specifies a maximum of approximately **30 cm** during the described handling operation.
- Operators responsible for installation, operation and maintenance must be skilled technicians familiar with gen-sets.
- Wear work gloves and safety shoes.
- Wear a safety helmet when the alternator or complete DG set is lifted from the floor.

### Installation stability

- Foundations and base frame must support the combined weight of alternator and prime mover.
- The alternator must be securely connected and perfectly aligned with the prime mover.
- Improper alignment can cause dangerous vibration and bearing failure.

### Environmental limits

The machine is designed for rated output when installed in a room with maximum temperature **40 °C** and altitude not exceeding **1000 m**. For deviations, refer to the manufacturer's technical documentation.

Do not wear loose or fluttering clothing near the machine.

The alternator must never run with the following guards removed:

- terminal box cover;
- rear cover;
- fan ventilation guard.

Alternators produce heat proportional to output. Do not touch the machine without suitable protection, and after switching off do not touch it until it has cooled.

Even if all machine components are protected, keep away from the gen-set and do not lean or sit on the alternator.

## 4.1 Danger of Short Circuit

The degree of protection of the alternator is **IP21**. Short circuits may occur if liquids such as water are splashed onto areas containing electrical parts.

## 4.2 Transport and Storage

Alternators are packed for shipment according to their transport mode and destination.

Before handling:

- Ensure lifting equipment has sufficient capacity.
- Ensure lifting conditions allow the machinery to be safely elevated.

Packed and unpacked alternators shall be stored in a cool, dry room and never exposed to inclement weather.

The generator may be coupled to an engine, mounted on a base frame, or installed as a complete gen-set. Follow the relevant lifting instructions.

Dispose of packing materials using correct waste-disposal methods.

## 4.3 Starting and Stopping Operations

All instrumentation required for starting, running and stopping the system shall be provided by the installer.

Starting, running and stopping operations must be carried out by skilled personnel who have read and understood the safety instructions in the manual.

When the system is set to work for the first time at reduced speed, check that no anomalous noises are detected. If abnormal noise occurs, stop the set immediately and correct the mechanical coupling.

## 4.4 Cleaning

Before approaching or touching the alternator, ensure that it is not live and is at room temperature.

- Clean the outside using compressed air.
- **Never use liquids or water for inside cleaning.**
- **Do not clean internal electrical components with compressed air**, because this may cause short circuits or other anomalies.

The alternator frame is provided with fixing holes so that vibration damping may be applied in the proper position.

## 4.5 Mechanical Coupling

> **CAUTION:** Alternator foot fixing on the side is on the end-shield foot. Care must be taken while closing the vent cover after tightening the bolt so that no material is left inside, which may cause fan damage during operation.

### 4.5.1 Assembly of AC Generator to Prime Mover — Foundation

The machine should be mounted on a foundation, which may be a steel structure or concrete platform. The foundation must provide adequate rigidity and long life while considering dynamic loading, abnormal running, vibration and other relevant factors.

The machine must be bolted down and levelled in all directions before mounting.

### 4.5.2 Single Bearing Generators

The rotor of a single-bearing generator is locked by a locking arrangement on the drive end to prevent damage during transportation. Remove the lock before coupling to the prime-mover engine.

Procedure:

1. Position and align the prime mover.
2. On the engine, measure the distance between the engine bell-housing mounting surface and the drive-disc recess in the engine flywheel. This is dimension `WB`.
3. Measure the distance from alternator end-shield spigot (adaptor spigot) face to the outside surface of the flexi-disc assembly. This is dimension `WA`.
4. Ensure the generator is parallel to its frame while measuring `WA`.
5. Measure the flexi-disc and engine flywheel drive-disc recess dimensions `C` and `C` as shown in the manual.
6. Check rotor position by verifying dimension `WA`; it should be equal to `WB`.
7. Measure the pitch-circle diameter of the fixing holes on the flexi-disc and engine flywheel drive-disc recess. Do not re-drill the holes.
8. Check engine flywheel run-out and flywheel housing run-out.
9. Support the generator using a hoist and move it horizontally towards the engine flywheel.
10. Ensure the flexi discs are properly seated within the drive-disc recess in the engine flywheel.
11. Fix bolts between the flexi disc and flywheel and tighten evenly.
12. Run the machine to full speed and check vibration.

### 4.5.3 Double Bearing Generators

- Place the prime mover on the bed plate and align it.
- Clean the drive-end shaft and fit the half coupling.
- Use the shaft key only for fixing the half coupling.
- Heat the half coupling before fitting it on the shaft.
- Avoid hammer blows.
- Cool the heated half coupling by blowing air; do not sprinkle water.
- Use the tapped hole at the shaft end to press in the half coupling.
- Position the generator on the base plate and adjust centre height, transverse and axial positions.
- Engage the generator coupling with the prime mover.
- Check alignment according to the coupling manufacturer's recommendations.
- Tighten the holding-down bolts after alignment.

# 5.0 Installation

Unpack the alternator and check the packing case for damage. If visible damage exists, the alternator itself may have been damaged. Report such damage to the transporter and insurance agents.

## 5.1.1 Location — Cooling

The ambient temperature shall not exceed **40 °C** at nominal ratings. For higher ambient temperatures, apply a derating factor.

Fresh air, free from humidity and dust, must circulate easily through the screen at the non-drive end. Avoid recycling heated air from the drive end or from the prime mover.

Ensure adequate ventilation and airflow at all times.

## 5.1.2 Operating in Particular Environmental Conditions

- At altitude above **1000 m**, derate by **4% for each 500 m** of increase.
- If ambient temperature exceeds **40 °C**, derate by **4% for each 5 °C** of increase.
- For sound-proof sets, ensure only fresh air enters the enclosure and position the alternator air inlet near and in front of the air opening.

# 5.2 Electrical Checks

Before putting the machine into service, an insulation-resistance check between phase and earth and between phases is recommended.

- Isolate each part being checked from the others.
- Use a **500 V DC megger**.
- Cold-machine insulation resistance should normally be **>10 MΩ**.

> **CAUTION:** A machine, new or used, should not be operated if insulation resistance is less than **1 MΩ** for the stator and other windings.

If the megger value is low, dry the machine until the minimum value is obtained.

Possible drying methods include:

1. bake the machine for 24 hours in an oven at **100 °C**;
2. dry the machine with a stream of hot air;
3. disconnect the CE unit (G2S series) or AVR (G2R series);
4. short-circuit the three output terminals through suitable rated-current connections and monitor current with an ammeter;
5. connect a **48 V storage battery** to the exciter field winding terminals, respecting polarity, in series with a rheostat of approximately **20 Ω, 250 W**;
6. open the alternator apparatus, terminal-box panels and protection screens;
7. start the gen-set at rated speed and adjust excitation through the rheostat to obtain rated short-circuit current; run under this condition for approximately 30 minutes to obtain approximately **1 MΩ** insulation resistance.

## 5.3 Direction of Rotation

The alternator may be driven in either direction, but the standard phase sequence is **U-V-W** when rotating clockwise viewed from the drive end.

For anticlockwise rotation, interchange phases **V and W** to obtain the U-V-W phase sequence.

# 8.0 Maintenance

## 8.1 Cooling Circuit

Check that cooling-air circulation is not restricted.

## 8.2 Bearings

The alternator bearings are self-lubricated and do not require maintenance for approximately **15,000 hours** of operation.

## 8.3 Voltage Setting and Revolving Speed

Check alternator output voltage at the nominal revolving speed of **1500 rpm**.

### 8.3.1 Slipring Alternators

Alternators are normally preset at the factory to supply a voltage slightly higher than nominal, typically **3–5%**, at nominal speed.

For G2S 132/160/200 frames, no-load voltage can be adjusted by operating on the air gap of the compound transformer:

1. Loosen screws `N`.
2. Regulate the air gap:
   - increasing the air gap increases voltage;
   - narrowing the air gap decreases voltage.
3. If voltage variation required exceeds 5%, replace insulating paper `T` of the air gap.
4. After setting voltage, tighten screws `N`.

Voltage can additionally be adjusted by changing capacitor taps. Moving taps from 1 toward 9 decreases voltage and vice versa.

For single-phase slipring alternators, the CE unit provides a tap for voltage setting.

### 8.3.2 Brushless Alternators

For alternators with an electronic voltage regulator, no-load voltage can be adjusted using the `VOLT` potentiometer of the AVR.

## 8.4 Remote Regulation of Voltage — Brushless

Remote voltage adjustment of approximately **±5%** is possible with a **100 kΩ potentiometer**. Refer to AVR connection diagrams.

> **WARNING:** For good alternator operation, do not deviate from nominal voltage by more than 5%.

## 8.5 Voltage Build-Up by Field Flashing at No Load

The alternator is self-exciting from residual magnetism in the exciter magnetic circuit.

For field flashing:

1. Connect a **12–24 V battery** to the terminals of the exciter field (main field winding for slipring types) for **2–3 seconds**.

> **CAUTION:** Ensure that the set is not running before field flashing. For G2R/G1R frames, exciter-field connections to the AVR must be removed.

## 8.6 Disassembly of Alternators

Before disassembly, read the safety requirements at the beginning of the manual.

1. Remove terminal-box cover.
2. For slipring alternator, disconnect the two field-winding terminals from the rectifier bridge.
3. For brushless alternator, disconnect the connections between winding and AVR.
4. Remove end cover.
5. Disconnect the two wires from the rotating rectifier to the main field.
6. Remove the rotating rectifier after removing its fastening screws.
7. Remove the diode-holding support by removing three M6 Allen screws.
8. Unscrew the three M8 screws fastening the exciter stator.
9. Remove exciter stator.
10. Use the three M6 tapped holes on the exciter rotor for removal with a suitable puller.
11. Unscrew DE end-shield fixing screws and remove the DE end shield.
12. Remove rotor from stator carefully to avoid damage to windings.
13. For slipring alternators, remove the brush-holder assembly before withdrawing the rotor.
14. Remove bearings using a suitable puller and use suitable pressing technique and mandrel for refitting.

### 8.6.1 Moulded Rectifier Bridge — Slipring Type Alternator

The moulded bridge rectifier is rated **25 A, 800 V**.

- Three-phase alternators use three-phase bridges.
- Single-phase alternators use single-phase bridges.

### 8.6.2 Rectifier Bridge Checkout

A diode can be checked with an ohmmeter or a battery and bulb.

A diode is working when:

- an ohmmeter shows very low resistance in one direction and very high resistance in the other; or
- with a battery and bulb, the bulb lights in one of the two possible connection directions.

# 8.7 Helpful Hints in Troubleshooting

For an electrical failure, determine whether the fault is in the alternator or in the regulation system by testing the alternator at no load and nominal speed while exciting the exciter stator directly from an external DC source.

Observe polarity when applying voltage:

- negative auxiliary-source terminal to negative exciter-field terminal;
- positive auxiliary-source terminal to positive exciter-field terminal.

Verify:

1. when the winding is fed according to the no-load excitation data, the alternator voltage is approximately rated voltage;
2. after locating the fault, repair it correctly.

## 8.8 Possible Faults — Initial Checks

If the alternator is not functioning correctly, first check:

- connections against the specified machine diagram;
- tightness of connections;
- prime-mover running speed using a frequency meter;
- correct setting of protection equipment.

# 12.0 Automatic Voltage Regulator — AVR SR 7/3 and UVR-6

## General

The PC board containing electronic components is located inside a box and embedded in elastomer resin. Terminals consist of 1/4-inch “Faston” lugs.

## Connections

Connection to the AVR should be made according to the schematic diagram in the manual.

## Potentiometer Description

| Potentiometer | Function |
|---|---|
| `VOLT` | Voltage adjustment |
| `STAB` | Stability adjustment |
| `Hz` | Under-speed protection adjustment |
| `AMP` | Over-excitation adjustment |

## Functions of AVR Potentiometers

### VOLT

Adjusts alternator terminal voltage.

- Clockwise rotation increases voltage.
- Counterclockwise rotation decreases voltage.

### STAB

Optimizes alternator response time.

- Counterclockwise: stability decreases, response becomes faster, voltage is less stable.
- Clockwise: response becomes slower, voltage becomes more stable.

Adjust the `STAB` potentiometer so that oscillations do not occur and damping is satisfactory.

### Hz

Adjusts intervention level of the low-frequency protection. This adjustment is normally performed by the manufacturer during testing and then sealed.

### AMP

Adjusts the intervention level of over-excitation protection. The protection system has a delay that permits temporary overload during motor starting or other purposes.

## Adjustment Procedure — Safety

> **CAUTION:** It is hazardous to carry out high-voltage tests on the alternator without first disconnecting all connections to the automatic voltage regulator (AVR). Damage to the AVR under such conditions is not covered by the stated warranty.

Alternators fitted with the particular AVR are tested and calibrated at the manufacturer's works. The following adjustment procedures are provided for commissioning or subsequent operation where required.

### Adjustment of `Hz` Potentiometer

1. Rotate the `Hz` potentiometer fully clockwise with the generator at nominal speed and no load.
2. Decrease rotating speed to the desired protection operating point — approximately **48 Hz for 50 Hz operation**.
3. Rotate the `Hz` potentiometer counterclockwise until voltage decreases by approximately **1.5%**.
4. Seal the `Hz` potentiometer.
5. Raise gen-set speed to nominal value.

### Adjustment of `AMP` Potentiometer

The `AMP` potentiometer is normally factory calibrated and sealed to establish the over-excitation protection threshold.

For calibration:

1. With alternator at no load and nominal speed, rotate `AMP` fully clockwise.
2. At full load, adjust `VOLT` until excitation voltage in DC, measured between regulator terminals 1 (-) and 3 (+), is **110% of nominal** at full load.
3. Record alternator output voltage.
4. Turn `AMP` fully counterclockwise.
5. After about 20 seconds, voltage should decrease considerably.
6. Immediately rotate `AMP` clockwise until alternator output voltage reaches a value approximately **2% lower** than the value recorded above.
7. Discharge the alternator to no load and adjust `VOLT` to restore rated output voltage.
8. Seal the `AMP` potentiometer.

## Remote Regulation of Voltage

A **100 kΩ potentiometer** connected between terminals **5 and 7** permits regulation of approximately **±5%** of nominal voltage.
